Why do we still learn from walls of text?

We have more information than any generation before us, and somehow understand less of it. Every big idea arrives the same way: a wall of text, a dozen tabs, a feed that moves on before we've caught up. So we skim. We nod along. We miss the point.

It wasn't always like this. For most of history, people learned by listening. Someone sat down and told the story: where it started, why it matters, what happens next. A good story doesn't just inform you. It stays with you.

Generative media gives us a chance to bring that back. Not to make more content, or louder content, but to make things make sense. One question, explained the way a patient teacher would. Researched, so it's true. Drawn, so you can see it. Narrated, so you can simply listen.

That's Romeo. Ask about anything: the news, a concept from class, the thing everyone at dinner was talking about. Romeo reads the sources, finds what matters, and turns it into a short animated story made just for you. When you're curious about something mid-story, ask, and the answer joins in.

We believe understanding should feel like being told a good story by a friend. We believe curiosity deserves better than a search result. And we believe the best stories are the ones you can share.

Ask anything. Get a story.

Ruchit Shah, designer and builder of Romeo
